Private Messaging and Secure Alternatives

CATEGORY: CYBERSECURITY / MESSAGING

[ ↓ RAW .ODT ]

Did you know that by using WhatsApp, Telegram or Instagram you are handing over the metadata of your conversations (who you talk to, at what time, from where and how often) to tech giants whose business model is tracking and monetizing your digital life? If you want to reclaim full control of your privacy, make the jump to secure encrypted alternatives like Signal, SimpleX Chat or Session.

The problem with traditional messaging

Traditional mass-market messaging platforms operate under schemes where user privacy takes a back seat to massive data collection:

  • Meta (WhatsApp and Instagram): Although WhatsApp uses end-to-end encryption (E2EE) for message content, Meta collects a massive amount of metadata (Rastogi and Hendler). They know who you are, who you communicate with, your location, your contact network and your usage habits. Instagram, for its part, does not even offer E2EE today in its direct chats (Dale).
  • Telegram: Contrary to common belief, Telegram does not encrypt your chats with E2EE by default. Everyday conversations and groups are stored on the company's servers, where they hold the keys to read them and share them with the Russian government (where the company is from). Only individual "secret chats" offer end-to-end encryption (Anin and Kondratyev).

Trusting these platforms exposes your social network and communication patterns to data leaks, massive legal requests, censorship and invasive advertising profiles.

The secure alternative: privacy by design

Adopting privacy-focused messaging applications means replacing this extractive model with one built on strong encryption, metadata minimization and free software, where you and your interlocutor are the only owners of the information transmitted.

A private messaging app with auditable free code guarantees that the software does exactly what it promises. Instead of tying your real life to a phone number or a centralized account, these technologies reduce or completely eliminate the need for personal identifiers.

Which apps should you use?

There are many cross-platform messaging applications, many that claim to be secure and a few that charge money, but few are trustworthy and even fewer have been audited. This is our list of secure applications.

  • Signal: This application has E2EE for all of its content; text, calls and files are encrypted on your device before they leave it. Signal keeps no logs of who you talk to or when (Cohn-Gordon et al.). Its only semi-mandatory point of contact is a phone number during registration, which is now optional with a one-time $2.99 USD payment via Signal Login (Signal).
  • Session: Also with E2EE, it requires no phone number or email to sign up. It uses a decentralized network of nodes (similar to Tor) to route your messages, hiding your IP address and ensuring that no central server knows who is talking to whom (Session).
  • SimpleX Chat: Another E2EE app with the highest level of privacy. Unlike the others, SimpleX assigns no usernames, numbers or fixed identifiers or IDs. It uses unidirectional transmission channels where the servers only see queues of encrypted messages without knowing whom they belong to (SimpleX Chat).

Why make the switch?

Metadata immunity: even if someone intercepted your network traffic or the platform's servers were attacked, there would be no usable records of your contacts or usage patterns; identity sovereignty: you can communicate without exposing real personal data, such as your mobile phone number; and free code and transparency: the code of these applications is publicly available so that independent cybersecurity experts can continuously verify that no backdoors exist.

How do I use Signal?

The simplest and most direct recommendation to start protecting your everyday communications is to install Signal.

  1. Download and install Signal from your device's official store (Android / iOS) or its official website (https://signal.org/download/).
  2. Create your account using your phone number or by paying for Signal Login.
  3. Configure your privacy: Go to Settings > Privacy and enable Username. Disable the option that lets others find you by your phone number to keep it private.
  4. Set a Signal PIN: This PIN protects your profile and your settings stored on the platform.
  5. Optional - Enable disappearing messages: In your important chats, enable automatic message deletion after a set time (e.g. 1 day or 1 week) to reduce the digital footprint on both devices.

How do I use Session and SimpleX Chat?

If you are looking for absolute anonymity or want to avoid linking your phone number:

  • Download Session (https://getsession.org), create an account by carefully storing your 12-word recovery phrase and share your alphanumeric ID with your contacts.
  • For maximum-security communications without fixed identities, use SimpleX Chat (https://simplex.chat/downloads/) and connect via QR codes or temporary links.

TL;DR

Traditional messaging apps can expose metadata about our contacts and habits. Signal, Session and SimpleX Chat offer more privacy through encryption, reduced data collection and open source code, though they require good configuration and responsible habits. Also, it is worth verifying and qualifying claims about Telegram and Signal's logging before drawing conclusions.
