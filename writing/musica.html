Owning Digital Music

CATEGORY: CULTURE / DIGITAL SOVEREIGNTY

[ ↓ RAW .ODT ]

A commercial music subscription service works in practice as a monitored rental environment: it collects telemetry in the background about your listening habits, forces algorithmic recommendations and dictates access to art based strictly on commercial licensing agreements. By using them, the user surrenders control of their library and is exposed to the sudden disappearance of their favorite albums and the unilateral decisions of large corporations.

Abandoning streaming services for buying music means changing this paradigm for a model of digital sovereignty and fair compensation for artists, guaranteeing total control over your audio files and true ownership of your library.

The advantages of owning your own music rest mainly on longevity and the absence of digital rights management (DRM). Unlike commercial platforms, local files do not report playback data to third parties or require a constant internet connection to verify your right to listen. To this is added total control and customization of your collection, where the user decides exactly what software to use, how it is organized and how it is backed up. On the other hand, it guarantees true independence from corporations by eliminating the risk that changes in a single company's business model block or condition access to your music.

Recommended Platforms for Buying Music

To build a sovereign digital library, it is essential to use platforms that let you download DRM-free files directly to your local hardware.

  • Bandcamp: A store focused on directly supporting artists, which allows unlimited downloads of purchased music in various restriction-free formats (including FLAC, ALAC and MP3).
  • HDTracks: A platform specialized in high-resolution digital files for audiophiles, completely free of the limitations imposed by proprietary ecosystems.
  • OTOTOY.jp: A Japanese purchase-focused service that offers high-fidelity lossless downloads, ensuring you keep a permanent digital file of independent and commercial releases.
  • Qobuz: Although widely known as a subscription service, it operates a robust digital store in certain regions where it also sells high-resolution DRM-free music files in addition to its streaming model.
