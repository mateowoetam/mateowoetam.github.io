Digital Sovereignty and Linux on the Desktop

CATEGORY: OPERATING SYSTEMS / PRIVACY

[ ↓ RAW .ODT ]

A commercial operating system (such as Windows or macOS) works in practice as a monitored rental environment: it collects telemetry in the background, forces updates, preinstalls bloatware and monitors device activity for commercial or advertising purposes. By using them, the user surrenders control of their hardware and is exposed to planned obsolescence and the unilateral decisions of large corporations.

Migrating to a desktop operating system based on Linux (GNU/Linux) means changing that paradigm for the free and open source software (FOSS) model, guaranteeing digital sovereignty, total control of the hardware and transparency over system activity.

Advantages of Linux on the Desktop

The advantages of Linux on the desktop rest mainly on the following pillars:

  • Privacy by design: Unlike commercial platforms, free distributions do not collect usage data, search histories or user profiles to sell them to third parties or feed tracking algorithms.
  • Total control and customization: The user decides exactly what software is installed, when the system is updated and how the visual interface is organized, eliminating bloatware.
  • Sovereignty and hardware longevity: It uses computing resources efficiently, which restores fluidity to old equipment and extends the useful life of the machine without artificial requirements.
  • Proactive security: It hinders the spread of malware, ransomware and silent vulnerabilities.
  • Corporate independence: Maintained by global communities under free licenses, eliminating the risk of unilateral corporate lockouts or restrictions.
  • Ecosystem of secure repositories: Applications are installed from centralized, audited sources, reducing the risk of malware downloaded from the web.
